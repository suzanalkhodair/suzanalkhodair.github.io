Editing Services

Manuscript Editing/Proofreading

Language review and refinement is recommended by most journal editors, who read hundreds of manuscripts and need to decide which to reject upfront and which to send for review. Don’t let avoidable language issues be the reason for an early rejection.

Your editor can help refine your manuscript by ensuring clarity, good grammar, correct spelling, punctuation, and word choice, as well as appropriate use of verb tense and voice. The editor will point out anything that needs to be clarified or that contradicts something written earlier, and will help to improve the narrative, flow and coherence of ideas, and consistency.

Oftentimes, manuscripts are written by a group of authors, where each author focuses on a single section. In these cases, there are certain issues to look out for, such as different writing styles, inconsistent tone, repetitions, and variable verb tense. Your editor can help by identifying and correcting these issues and unifying the paper using a consistent tone and style.

It is important to ensure that your manuscript follows the latest ICMJE Recommendations, which are updated frequently based on developing issues, such as AI use, transparency, access to research data, and careful referencing. You can access the latest update of the ICMJE Recommendations from the official website (https://www.icmje.org/). Translations into Arabic and other languages are also available.

Levels of Editing

There are different levels of editing; your editor will use the level suitable for each section. A full edit combines all of the following to refine your document’s structure, elevate its stylistic flow, and eliminate errors.

Copyediting is basic editing that focuses on grammar, punctuation, and word choice.

Substantive editing is a deeper level of editing that helps to improve structure, presentation of ideas, logical flow, and consistent use of terminology.

Quality assurance ensures consistency throughout the manuscript, including use of terminology, spelling, abbreviations, and headings.

Proofreading is an important last step in any editing project; the proofreader carefully inspects the manuscript, one word at a time, to pick up spelling errors, typos, punctuation mistakes, extra or missing spaces. This gives the manuscript a professional, polished look. Some manuscripts may only require proofreading.
